DARES: Data Quality for Responsible AI in Energy Systems


Data Quality for Responsible AI in Energy Systems (DARES) will investigate the relationship between energy systems, data and AI, focusing on the challenges of data quality for effective implementations of AI in support of the green transition. The Nordic region leads the world in the development and integration of renewable energy sources into sustainable energy systems, aiming to be fossil-free by 2050. It is clear that powerful AI systems offer significant potential for developing green energy solutions and achieving further ambitious carbon emission reduction goals in the region. Energy systems in the Nordic and Baltic countries are critical infrastructures and must be 100% reliable. The recent experience of the Iberian Peninsula, when the entire Spanish and Portuguese national grids went dark, demonstrates that energy security and reliability are pre-eminent issues. AI systems, on the other hand, are intrinsically non-deterministic and prone to failure in unexpected ways (Wala, 2024). One of the core reasons for AI failure is the lack of quality data (Balahur, 2022), resulting in risk of blackouts and other energy system failures. Responsible development and implementation of AI in energy systems require data quality.
The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AIA) categorises AI systems used in critical infrastructures as high-risk due to the grave consequences of system failure or mismanagement (Wala, 2024). AIA requires that high-risk systems are well-made, well-maintained and appropriately controlled (Sousa Silva, 2025). Machine learning systems are subject to requirements for high quality training data, obligations for oversight, and ensuring quality of operational data for systems in use (AI Act Art 10, Art 26). Surprisingly, despite decades of research and standards development, data quality remains an underdefined and inconsistently operationalised concept (Mohammed et al, 2024). This is in part because data quality is a qualitative and socio-technical concept, only partially definable through metrics and measurement (Leese, 2022), since data, are made by people (Zajac et al., 2023).
For the Nordic-Baltic Region to achieve the vision of becoming the most sustainable and integrated region in the world by 2030, the problem of data quality must be addressed. Policy makers and regulators need clear definitions and an understanding of the socio-technical issues surrounding data quality. Energy industries, from heating to power, recognise data quality as an acute issue and require clear guidelines for how to create conditions for high-quality data creation and use. DARES will address these needs through the following objectives:

  • WP1: Evaluate and compare AI-driven green transition policy initiatives and regulatory concerns across partner countries
  • WP2: Develop usable quality criteria and guidelines for the creation of energy data and its use in analytics and AI system development through comparative research of data practices across several partner countries
  • WP3: Explore production and evaluation of synthetic datasets in practice, develop a synthetic dataset modelling electrical vehicle (EV) based mobility patterns as a test case
  • WP4: Develop and test the quality-aware representation learning (QARL) method to incorporate broader data quality considerations into standard representational learning methods, to incorporate data quality into building AI responsibly
  • WP5: Develop the speculative futures workshop method as a radical approach to weaving a speculative Nordic-Baltic future vision for responsible AI in energy systems
